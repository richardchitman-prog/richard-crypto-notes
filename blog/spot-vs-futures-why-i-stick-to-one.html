Spot vs. Futures: Why I Stick to One and Don't Touch the Other

October 11, 2026

Someone asked me last week why I never talk about futures trading when I post about crypto. Fair question. I talk about charts, I talk about entries and exits, I talk about watching the market — but I never mention leverage or contracts or shorting. That's not an oversight. It's a choice, and I think it's worth explaining why.

The basic difference

Spot trading is the simple version: you buy the actual asset. You own some Bitcoin, or some Ethereum, or whatever it is, and its value goes up or down with the market. If the price drops, you lose on paper, but you still hold the coin. Nothing gets taken from you unless you sell.

Futures trading is different. You're not buying the asset — you're entering a contract that bets on where the price is going, often using borrowed money (leverage) to make the bet bigger than your actual account balance. If you're right, the gains are amplified. If you're wrong, the losses are too, and they can come faster than you can react. People get liquidated — meaning their entire position gets wiped out — in a matter of minutes during a sharp move.

Why I only do one

I'm not going to pretend I've never been curious about futures. The idea of amplifying a good call is appealing, in theory. But when I'm honest with myself about how I actually handle risk, I know spot is the right lane for me.

Here's the thing about leverage: it doesn't just amplify your gains, it amplifies your emotional response to every candle. I already know I can get a little too invested in watching price movement on spot trades, where the worst case is "I own less value than I did yesterday." I have no interest in finding out what my stress response looks like when the worst case is "my position just evaporated because the market moved 4% in the wrong direction while I was asleep."

That's not a moral stance on futures. People manage it responsibly — I just don't think I'm one of them, and I'd rather admit that than find out the hard way.

What this actually looks like day to day

  • I only buy what I can afford to hold through a bad stretch, because on spot, time is usually on your side.
  • I don't use borrowed money to trade, period.
  • If a position goes against me, the most I lose is what I put in — never more.
  • I sleep fine, which matters more to me now than it used to.

None of this means spot trading is "safe." Crypto is volatile no matter which way you trade it, and I've had plenty of red days holding spot positions. But there's a real difference between losing value on something you own and losing a position you never actually controlled in the first place, because the math behind it worked against you faster than you could act.

Know your own wiring

I think the honest version of risk tolerance isn't a number you pick off a quiz. It's knowing how you behave when things move against you — and building your trading around that reality instead of the version of yourself you wish you were. For me, that means spot, full stop.
