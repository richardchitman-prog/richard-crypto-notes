Field Notes on Crypto

The Beginner's Glossary I Wish Someone Had Handed Me

October 25, 2026

When I started looking into crypto, the vocabulary was the first wall I hit. Not the trading, not the charts — just the words. People would toss around "gas fees" or "cold wallet" like everyone already knew what they meant, and nobody ever stopped to define them. So I started keeping a running list of terms I had to look up, sometimes more than once because they didn't stick the first time. Here's that list, cleaned up a bit.

Wallet

Not a physical thing. A wallet is software (or hardware) that holds the keys proving you own a certain amount of crypto. The crypto itself lives on the blockchain — the wallet just holds the proof and lets you move it.

Hot wallet vs. cold wallet

A hot wallet is connected to the internet — an app or browser extension. Convenient, but more exposed. A cold wallet is offline, usually a physical device. Slower to use, harder to hack. I treat it like the difference between cash in my pocket and cash in a safe.

Seed phrase

A string of random words (usually 12 or 24) that acts as the master backup for your wallet. If you lose your device but have the seed phrase, you can recover everything. If someone else gets the seed phrase, they get everything too. This is the one term I wish had come with a warning label.

Public key / address

Think of this as your account number — the thing you give someone so they can send you crypto. Safe to share.

Private key

The thing you never share. It's what actually authorizes a transaction. Your seed phrase is basically a human-readable backup of your private keys.

Gas fee

This one confused me the most. A gas fee is the cost of having your transaction processed by the network. You're not paying a company — you're paying the computers (nodes) that verify and record the transaction. Fees change based on how busy the network is, which is why the same transaction can cost very different amounts depending on the time of day.

Gwei

A tiny unit of a cryptocurrency, usually mentioned in the context of gas fees on Ethereum. One gwei is a billionth of one ETH. You don't need to do the math yourself — most wallets show you the fee in regular currency — but it helps to know the word isn't some separate coin.

Confirmation

A transaction isn't instantly "done." It needs to be confirmed by the network, which can take anywhere from seconds to minutes depending on the blockchain and how much gas you paid. Higher gas, generally faster confirmation.

Slippage

The difference between the price you expected and the price you actually got when a trade executes. Comes up a lot in fast-moving or low-liquidity markets.

Why this mattered to me

None of these terms are complicated once someone explains them plainly. The problem is that most places assume you already know them, so you end up nodding along and quietly feeling behind. I didn't want to keep doing that, so I wrote this list mostly for myself — and figured it might save someone else the same round of late-night searching.
