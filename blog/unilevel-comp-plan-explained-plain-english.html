So what does "unilevel" actually mean?

Strip away the buzzwords and it's pretty simple. In a unilevel structure, everyone you personally refer sits on your "level one" — a single row, side by side. It doesn't matter how many people you bring in; they all land on that same first level, not stacked underneath each other in some narrow chain.

Then, when those people refer others, those new people become your level two. Their referrals become your level three, and so on, down through a set number of levels depending on the company's plan. The width is unlimited — you can have as many people on level one as you're able to bring in — but the depth is usually capped at a certain number of levels.

That's really the whole mechanic. No binary trees, no forced matrices, no weird placement rules where a new person might get slotted under someone else's leg to "help" them. It's a flat, wide structure that pays out based on levels of depth rather than complicated placement math.

Why this matters when someone's explaining it to you

I think the reason these plans get a bad reputation is that people explain them badly — or don't explain them at all. So let me just say it directly: iCoinPro pays affiliates through a unilevel structure. That's the mechanic. Nobody should have to guess at that or piece it together from a slide deck.

What a unilevel plan does not tell you is how much anyone actually earns doing it, and I'm not going to pretend to know that or suggest a number, because it depends entirely on the person, the effort, and honestly a fair bit of circumstance. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something other than the truth.
