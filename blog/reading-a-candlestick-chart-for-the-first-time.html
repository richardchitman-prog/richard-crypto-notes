Reading a Candlestick Chart for the First Time (Here's What Confused Me)

September 13, 2026

The first time I opened a crypto chart, I closed the tab within about thirty seconds. Red and green blocks stacked on top of little lines, numbers scrolling by, no idea what any of it meant. If that's you right now, this post is the one I wish someone had written for me.

I'm not a trader by background. I came into this from the education side, trying to understand the tools before I ever talked about them. So take this as one beginner's notes, not expert advice.

What a single candle is actually showing you

Here's the part that finally made it click for me: each candlestick is just a tiny story about price during one time period — could be one minute, one hour, one day, depending on what you set the chart to.

  • The thick part (the "body") shows the opening and closing price for that period.
  • The thin lines sticking out the top and bottom (the "wicks") show the highest and lowest price it touched.
  • The color tells you direction — usually green (or white) means price closed higher than it opened, red (or black) means it closed lower.

Once I understood that each candle was a self-contained snapshot of a fight between buyers and sellers over a set window of time, the chart stopped looking like noise.

What confused me at first

A few things tripped me up early on, and I suspect they trip up most people:

  • Timeframes reset everything. A chart set to "1 hour" candles looks completely different from the same asset set to "1 day" candles. I kept comparing charts without realizing I was looking at different timeframes and getting confused about why they didn't match.
  • Long wicks felt scarier than they needed to be. A candle with a huge wick just means price swung a lot during that period before settling somewhere else. It's information, not a warning siren.
  • I assumed one candle told a full story. It doesn't. A single green candle doesn't mean "good," and a single red one doesn't mean "bad." It's the pattern across several candles — and the context around them — that starts to mean something.
  • I wanted a shortcut pattern to memorize. There are named patterns (doji, hammer, engulfing, and so on) and they're worth learning eventually, but starting there before understanding the basic body-and-wick structure just added confusion on top of confusion for me.

How I actually got comfortable with it

Honestly, it was repetition more than anything. I'd pull up a chart, cover the price numbers, and try to guess what happened — did it open higher and drop, or open low and climb — then check myself. Slow, unglamorous, but it worked better than reading ten articles about candlestick theory.

I also found it easier once I stopped trying to "predict" anything and just focused on describing what I saw. That took the pressure off.
